## HR-001 — Authority to establish policy-admission requirements

**Status:** Resolved at semantic level; accepted for incorporation on 2026-09-28.
Implementation realization remains deferred. The narrow amendment is applied;
its focused Pro completeness audit is complete with no qualifying P1/P2 findings.

**Question resolved:** How do admission requirements first become authoritative,
and who or what may establish, activate, and replace them? This concerns authority
over those requirements, not authorship or admission of an individual candidate.

Following the [focused Pro decision review](../../../research/hcm-admission-rule-authority-pro-decision-review-2026-09-28.md),
the user accepted this item as specified and requested its narrow incorporation
into the four parts and decision material, followed by a thorough audit of the
amendment's completeness. This acceptance is limited to HR-001/P2-01; it is not
whole-package approval or authorization to implement the proposed system.

**Accepted semantics:**

- A fixed, versioned Handbook establishment rule supplies the initial authority
  outside the project policy-candidate population. In the supported standalone
  mode, an explicit local instruction or configured agent acting under it may
  supply honestly advisory authority. Missing established governance does not
  authorize reinitialization.
- A separately attributable project admission-governance responsibility establishes
  the exact configuration, project/scope, applicable owner rules, active version,
  and history. Domain owners retain validation and ordinary candidate admission.
  This does not require a separate service, component, or authored artifact family.
- Deliberate successor activation uses previously established replacement
  authority and the exact current predecessor. The successor cannot authorize
  itself. Saving or validating draft content does not activate it.
- A new admission finalizes against the governing configuration and owner rules
  active at its decision boundary; changed governance refuses stale-basis
  finalization. Completed decisions retain their exact historical basis.
- Replacement alone neither revokes nor re-admits existing policies or establishes
  successor-rule compliance. Present usability follows exact owner and consumer
  currentness, compatibility, scope, and dependency rules.
- Activation follows a bounded governance lifecycle that avoids circular reliance
  on project policies it is establishing. Validation, permission, and committed
  effect remain distinct. Other evaluation and operational boundaries still apply.
- A Decision Record is optional rationale. No identity platform, login, additional
  human confirmation, or independent identity claim is required by this decision.

**Where incorporated:** [Shared policy admission](objects-and-governance.md#policy-admission)
owns establishment/replacement, provenance, cutover, refusals, and examples. Its
ontology and responsibility map classify configurations and owner outcomes. The
other three parts inherit the governing basis and preserve their domain boundaries;
the action part explicitly scopes the setup lifecycle. Principal decision 2 in the
approval guide summarizes this clarification.

**Remaining implementation choices:** Configuration representation and concrete
project requirements, APIs, physical owner placement, storage, identity encoding,
activation/concurrency and recovery mechanics. These choices cannot change the
accepted authority, scope, cutover, history, or refusal semantics.

**Verification:** Local cross-document review and the completed
[focused Pro amendment audit](../../../research/hcm-admission-rule-amendment-pro-audit-2026-09-28.md)

## HR-003 — Action authorization must not imply a new identity platform

**Status:** Resolved explanation concern; editorial incorporation completed and
locally reviewed on 2026-09-30. Raised 2026-09-27; originally principal decision 10,
now decision 11 after the addition of complete pruning. The fresh final audit
completed on 2026-09-30 and confirms HR-003 may remain resolved.

The 2026-09-27 comprehensive audit independently supports this interpretation.
It identifies no requirement for a universal login, permission service, or additional
human approval prompt. The separate action result and execution safeguards remain.

**Concern:** The chat explanation that a developer's CLI "still needs permission"
and cannot merely type an approver's name may encourage implementation of a complex
authorization system that is not intended. It foregrounds a conditional stronger
identity requirement as though that were the ordinary or mandatory path.

**Verified in the four parts:**

- [Objects and governance](objects-and-governance.md#ontology) classifies the
  Action Authorization Binding as an owner result, not inherently a credential,
  authenticated identity, capability token, or proof of host enforcement. Its
  action-authority responsibility explicitly excludes requiring a general IAM platform.
- [Assurance and evaluation preparation](assurance-and-evaluation-preparation.md#advisory-and-enforceable-behavior)
  permits policy-authorized prompt/configured-role advisory action authority and
  does not impose identity, credential, authentication, delegation, secrets, or
  role/attribute access-control infrastructure. Evaluation authorization remains
  a distinct responsibility.
- [Evidence and evaluation](evidence-and-evaluation.md#metric-gate) preserves the
  separate action result and truthful advisory/verified/enforced provenance; its
  responsibility map rejects interpreting this as a general Handbook IAM mandate.
- [Operational reliance and actions](operational-reliance-and-actions.md#authority-provenance-without-a-handbook-iam-platform)
  expressly permits advisory authority to support an actual state-changing attempt
  when the exact policy permits it for that action and target. Authentication and
  enforcement mechanisms belong to systems that actually provide them. Its paired
  scenarios cover both permitted advisory authority and refusal when a different
  policy explicitly requires stronger facts.

**Clarification to preserve:** A separate authorization decision for the exact action
does not by itself require a separate human approval prompt, account system, login,
permission service, or cryptographic credential. It also does not make every prompt
sufficient: the governing rule must permit the supplied authority basis. Required
eligibility, exact target/effect, currentness, attempt, and commit checks still apply.
"Advisory" describes the strength of the authority evidence here, not a restriction
to suggestions or dry runs. Stronger verified/enforced facts are conditional on
the selected policy, not a universal default. No concrete default policy is selected.

**Incorporated example:** A task meets the conditions for closure. The user
asks an agent to close that exact task. If the applicable action rule permits this
user instruction as advisory authority, the system can record and proceed on that
basis, subject to the exact target and execution checks. It must not claim it
independently authenticated the user or verified a delegation that it did not check.
A separate login or approval service is not required merely by this semantic decision.

**Disposition:** The guide now leads with permitted instruction/configured-role
authority for actual effects, with no inherent extra login, approval prompt, or
identity service. Stronger verification is conditional on the applicable policy;
truthful provenance and all exact action/target/execution checks remain required.
Preserve these limits during implementation planning. No contradictory mandate
was identified in the checked passages. This resolves an
explanation/drift concern, not approval of a new authority model or removal of the
separate authorization result. Any proposal for additional identity or permission
infrastructure needs its own concrete requirement; it cannot be inferred from the
word "authorization" alone. HR-001 now records the accepted establishment/replacement boundary; it does not
change these action-authority limits.
