## The complete path

The following is a reading map. Each arrow crosses the exact admission,
identity, currentness, and refusal boundary defined in the owning part; it is
not permission to skip a stage or a declaration that every stage always occurs.

```text
owner-established complete definitions + exact governance applicability and required project qualification
  -> complete Evaluation Requirements from either:
       scoped posture and assurance selection, OR direct contract resolution
  -> prospective applicability / observation plan
  -> permitted-use compatibility / purpose including occurrence identity
  -> Engine evaluation authorization + capability facts for actual selections
  -> readiness and attempt disposition
  -> Prospective Evaluation Basis, if attemptable
  -> resolve governing target/assessment relationship before commitment for the prospectively governed protocol under G5
  -> ordinary collection commitment, attempts/terminals, and sealed frontier
       OR complete-pruning commitment and sealed empty frontier in one owner outcome
  -> canonical evidence-set closure and final evaluation context
  -> immutable applicability / claim / metric / gate / explanation truth
  -> resolve current governing requirements/protocol and verify assessment coverage or qualify exact earlier results under G5, when applicable
  -> action-specific Operational Reliance Basis, if present use is requested
  -> one atomic Determination: eligibility + fixed disposition, or Refusal
  -> congruent Action Intent, only after eligible / allow
  -> separate Action Authorization Binding or Refusal
  -> exact Action Attempt and Terminal
  -> authoritative Mutation Receipt, only for a committed mutation
```

When exact admitted rules over independent pre-existing facts positively prove
every required claim inapplicable, the supported **complete-pruning route** may
close collection without running a producer. It retains the original nonempty
requirements and observation inventory, every exclusion proof, real authorization,
complete empty-evidence accounting, and final verification. Closing permission
and readiness checks go into the exact basis committed; a required newer check
before commitment follows the bounded reformation rule, leaving earlier bases
unused. An empty plan because tools are unavailable does not qualify. Initially
empty obligations and retained observation-free claims are outside this route.
No observations means neither tests passed nor permission to close work.

While ordinary collection remains open, each later producer launch or permitted
retry gets its required fresh checks against the same committed evaluation basis.
Those checks are recorded separately, including failures and any permitted
recovery. Only operational choices allowed by the original rules may vary;
changed fixed inputs require the applicable new evaluation chain. A new timestamp
does not rewrite the basis, and a successful recheck cannot reopen sealed
collection or reset retry limits. See [later launch verification](evidence-and-evaluation.md#post-commit-verification).

Posture feedback follows the same forward order: an exact historical result may
be admitted as a Posture Observation, support a separate recommendation, and
then enter transition-specific reliance and the separate action chain. It cannot
rewrite the evaluation's original posture or mutate posture by itself.

An owner-established complete contract is either **Diagnostic-only** or **Decision-seeking eligible**.
These describe allowed run intent, not quality, maturity, or project approval.
Changing the value requires a newly admitted revision. Requirements may come
directly from an exact contract or from project posture; both routes must close
the complete rules before execution. A pass answers only that exact question.

These run-intent values do not establish project qualification. Complete direct contracts may be evaluated during positive governance deferral under G4–G5.

For governed completion, the action owner independently identifies the actual
project standard, target and supported adopted assessment protocol. Mandatory
positive roles require original Decision-seeking eligible/decision-seeking truth.
A prospective protocol requires governing selection before collection or
no-collection commitment and posture-selected requirements. An expressly adopted
retrospective protocol may accept an exact earlier direct result through new
current owner qualification under G5. Neither setup nor a pass supplies assignment
or acceptance. Diagnostic results cannot substitute, and all required coverage,
process conditions, separate authority and through-commit checks remain.

Example: Diagnostic-only C1 checks response shape; its findings inform C2 and a
later eligible C3. C3 still must be admitted and selected under the actual project
requirements before the required completion assessment runs. Earlier findings
remain useful history; their evidence and passes are not transferred. This is
an example, not a mandatory sequence every contract must follow.

## Principal decisions to approve

These explanations cover the original eleven decisions plus the complete-pruning
decision added during review. Examples illustrate the rules; they do not choose
project policies or implementation. An **owner** means the part of Handbook
responsible for establishing a particular fact, not necessarily a person, separate
service, or extra approval step. A **contract** defines what is checked and the
rules for interpreting observations.

### 1. Documents, test reports, and decisions establish different things

A project document records intent; a test producer reports what it observed;
the evaluation applies the contract's rules to decide what that observation proves.
Each is authoritative only for its own bounded fact. Saving a file, accepting a
document, or checking its format does not give it all those roles.

**Example:** A work document says “completed.” That records the document's content;
it does not prove the component managing task state actually closed the task.

Details: [object roles](objects-and-governance.md#ontology) and
[document relationships](objects-and-governance.md#authored-relationships).

### 2. Establish policy-acceptance requirements before accepting a proposed policy

Handbook supplies an exact starting establishment rule and owner conformance
floors. People approve initial project/Charter/posture choices and delegated
boundaries, choosing project governance now or deliberately deferring it. When
selected, governance activates exact versioned qualification requirements and
replacement authority. Supplied choices are recommendations or replaceable
defaults, not automatic project policy. Agents can apply approved rules without
repeated prompts when their authority and required facts resolve.

Project qualification must be established before dependent governed uses succeed;
intrinsic owner establishment of complete contract/evaluation definitions does
not inherently need it. Drafting, validating, qualifying, assigning a governing
role, and authorizing an action are separate. Replacement cannot authorize itself.

**Example:** An agent proposes “Skip security tests for documentation-only changes.”
Handbook checks the proposal against the established project requirements and
domain rules, including any required approval. Missing inputs or failed checks
prevent activation. The proposal cannot make itself acceptable by supplying its
own easier acceptance criteria.

Details: [policy admission and project setup](objects-and-governance.md#policy-admission).

### 3. Decide what must be proved before checking whether tools can prove it

Requirements may be selected from project settings or resolved directly from a
particular owner-established complete contract. Both routes need complete rules for that evaluation.
Project **posture** means scoped settings across separate dimensions, rather than
one overall strictness score. **Assurance selection** uses those settings and
accepted policies to choose the required proof. Tool capability is compared with
that proof afterward; inability to obtain it cannot silently reduce requirements.

Its independent check can produce real results while governance is deliberately deferred, with exact applicability provenance. It cannot satisfy a later governed obligation by relabeling the result.

A **Diagnostic-only** contract permits diagnostic runs only. **Decision-seeking
eligible** means the contract may be considered for a run intended to support a
decision, not that it passed or was selected by the project. Changing this
designation requires a newly accepted revision. Direct results can fill declared current roles only after independently adopted G5 qualification; diagnostic runs cannot fill the mandatory positive assessment role. Required prospective provenance cannot be supplied retrospectively.

**Example:** An early contract checks response shape and passes. The project's
completion standard also requires security checks. The early pass helps development
but cannot satisfy that standard. Unavailable security tools do not remove it.

Details: [posture and requirements](assurance-and-evaluation-preparation.md#posture-assurance),
[contract permitted use](objects-and-governance.md#contract-permitted-use), and
[governed completion](operational-reliance-and-actions.md#governed-completion).

### 4. Permission, ability, operating limits, readiness, and enforcement are separate

**Evaluation authorization** is Engine's permission for this exact check on this
subject and scope. **Capability** describes what the producer can observe. An
adapter's **operating ceiling** is the limit it promises to respect. **Readiness**
compares the required proof with the authorized plan and valid current facts to
support the decision whether an attempt can proceed. **Host enforcement** records
restrictions actually applied by the running environment. None proves a test passed.

**Example:** A scanner may have permission to inspect a repository but lack the
ability to check a required behavior. That limitation is not a completed check.
Sending “read-only” to the scanner also does not prove its host prevented writes.
A diagnostic run may preserve valid known limitations, but it still needs valid
authorization and the other required inputs.

Details: [evaluation preparation](assurance-and-evaluation-preparation.md#evaluation-preparation).

### 5. Receiving a report twice is different from running a test twice

Repeated delivery of the same report does not create another execution. Actually
running a test again creates a separate attempt, even with identical output. An
originally permitted retry can stay within the same evaluation; a deliberately
new evaluation receives its own identity even with identical inputs. Neither
extra delivery nor another attempt automatically adds evidentiary weight.

Once collection commits to an evaluation's exact input basis, later launch checks
are recorded separately. They cannot rewrite that basis, hide failures, extend
retry allowances, or reopen sealed collection. Only originally permitted operational
choices may vary; changing fixed inputs requires the applicable new evaluation chain.

**Example:** Service A sends report R twice: one execution is represented. A permitted
rerun creates attempt B and its own report, with its fresh checks recorded alongside
A's history. Changing the contract's pass threshold instead requires a new evaluation.

Details: [identity and evidence](evidence-and-evaluation.md#identity-evidence) and
[later launch checks](evidence-and-evaluation.md#post-commit-verification).

### 6. Running no observations requires proof that every required claim is inapplicable

This supported route starts with actual requirements and observation obligations.
Accepted rules over independent existing facts must positively prove every required
claim inapplicable before all observations are removed. Permission, complete
accounting, and final verification still apply. One recorded outcome commits the
final checked basis and seals empty collection, without inventing test runs.
Arbitrary empty contracts and retained observation-free claims are outside this route.

**Example:** Suppose a contract applies solely to one component, and its rules accept
an exact, independently established change inventory as proof that component is
unaffected. If that proves every required claim inapplicable, no producer runs;
“tests passed” would be false. A separate requirement to scan for hidden changes
must still be honored. A work description or unavailable tool is not such proof.

Details: [complete pruning](evidence-and-evaluation.md#complete-pruning).

### 7. Findings, measurements, and verdicts answer different questions

A **claim finding** says what evidence establishes about a requirement. A **metric**
calculates a value under specified rules. A **gate** decides its stated question
from the required inputs. Invalid or missing inputs that prevent a valid evaluation
cause refusal to form a result, not an ordinary failed or uncertain result. Short
summaries and detailed explanations must remain views of the same recorded truth.

**Example:** A score of 99/100 cannot override a gate rule separately requiring a
security condition to pass. A concise “blocked” display may link to the detailed
explanation, but cannot replace its underlying findings with another verdict.

Details: [claims, metrics, gates, and explanation](evidence-and-evaluation.md#metric-gate).

### 8. A valid past result does not automatically qualify for use today

Before using a result to decide whether an action qualifies now, resolve its exact
history. For results that bind Engine evaluation authorization, verify validity at
the required historical checkpoints. The applicable rule must also choose whether
that evaluation permission needs to remain current: **historical_validity_only**
requires historical validity; **continuing_current** adds a fresh current check.
Other required target, scope, and current-condition checks still apply in either
mode. Authorization to perform the action is a separate decision.

**Example:** Evaluation permission expires after a valid test result was closed.
The old result stays unchanged. One permitted action rule may still accept it after
other required checks; another requires continuing permission and refuses without
it. Neither rule authorizes the state change by itself.

Details: [present reliance on results](operational-reliance-and-actions.md#reliance-actions).

### 9. Whether an action qualifies and the instruction that follows must agree

One decision records both answers: qualifies means **allow**; does not qualify means
**block**; a valid unresolved answer means **hold**; a valid finding that the action
does not apply means **no effect**. If required inputs cannot be established, the
decision cannot be formed; that refusal is not an ordinary hold. “Allow” here does
not supply the separate authorization to perform the action.

**Example:** A valid decision says a task does not qualify for closure. Its
control-facing output must say “block,” not “allow.” If a required test record cannot
be resolved at all, report that formation failure instead of inventing eligibility.

Details: [eligibility and disposition](operational-reliance-and-actions.md#reliance-actions).

### 10. Qualifying to close a task does not close it or qualify its parent

The proposed **Work Progression** responsibility decides local and parent eligibility
using exact work relationships and required results. It does not change task state
or rewrite test truth. For governed completion the action owner independently resolves the actual target, requirements and adopted protocol. Prospective assessment resolves the governing relationship before collection/no-collection commitment; retrospective acceptance uses new current qualification of exact earlier results under G5. Mandatory roles retain original Decision-seeking eligible/decision-seeking provenance. Both routes require current complete coverage through commit and separate action authority.

**Example:** A child task qualifies for closure. Its parent requires that child
actually closed plus another child complete. The first child's eligibility satisfies
neither condition by itself. Even after local closure, the parent's own requirements
must still be checked.

Details: [governed completion and Work Progression](operational-reliance-and-actions.md#governed-completion)
and the selected [kinds, relations and completion amendment](amendments/work-progression-kinds-and-relations.md).

### 11. Authorizing an action need not require a new login or approval service

The system separately authorizes the exact action and target. When the applicable
rule permits it, a user's instruction or configured agent role may supply **advisory
authority**: a declared permission basis that has not necessarily been independently
authenticated. It may permit a real state change; “advisory” does not restrict it
to suggestions or dry runs. Eligibility, target/effect, currentness, and execution
checks remain required.

**Example:** A task qualifies for closure and the user asks an agent to close it.
If the applicable action rule permits that instruction, Handbook can record the
authority and proceed through execution checks without another login or approval
prompt. It must not claim it independently verified identity or delegation. A
different rule may require stronger proof; this model does not make that universal
or make every prompt sufficient.

Details: [authority without a general identity platform](operational-reliance-and-actions.md#authority-provenance-without-a-handbook-iam-platform).

### 12. An allowed or attempted action does not prove the change committed

The attempt and its reported ending are separate from authoritative confirmation
that the intended state change took effect. That confirmation comes from the owner
of the affected state. A successful transport call or timeout cannot substitute
for it. Uncertain commit prevents ordinary retry until authoritative non-application
is established or a separately permitted recovery rule accounts for possible prior
success and supplies the required fresh authorization.

**Example:** A close-task request times out after being sent. The task may already
be closed. Do not report failure as certain or blindly repeat a non-idempotent
change; resolve the state owner's outcome or follow the declared duplicate-safe
recovery path.

Details: [attempts, outcomes, and mutation receipts](operational-reliance-and-actions.md#reliance-actions).

## Current product versus proposed model

The [implemented baseline](../implemented-baseline.md) and completed HCM records
document prior delivery and owner direction; current implementation claims must
be checked against current source before implementation planning. They do not
impose compatibility on this greenfield model, and this package does not claim

The register separately tracks stale summaries in `architecture-context.md` and
an overbroad Dock-absence phrase in `seam-crosswalk.md`. Those summaries are not
a verified inventory of the current implementation. Reconcile them against source,
exports, and manifests before using them as planning premises. This semantic review
does not establish runtime conformance or reopen completed slices.

No V1 shape, legacy name or alias, migration, compatibility bridge, dual-write,
or dual behavior is selected. `Work Specification` remains the canonical
greenfield artifact family; `FeatureSpec` is not a seventh family.

## Explicitly deferred

Each chapter retains its detailed settled/deferred boundary. Across the package,
the following remain unselected:

- Concrete policies, selectors, mappings, requirement sets, contract-use qualification
  criteria, governing project/effect/target mappings, exception and actor rules,
  metrics, thresholds, algorithms, producer choices, and result populations.
  Dependent admissions and completion operations cannot succeed without their
  required exact qualification and governing relationships.
- Concrete realization of the [selected work composition and relation rules](amendments/work-progression-kinds-and-relations.md#selected-kinds-and-hierarchy),
  work/parent state machines and operations, reopen/cancel/removal consequences,
  physical ownership placement, mutation effects, and concrete authorization
  mechanisms. Initial containment cardinality and prerequisite timing are selected
  semantic constraints; multi-parent and external relations remain later choices.
- Schemas, fields, types, APIs, operation names, storage and repositories,
  registries/resolvers, identity-allocation and clock technology, retention
  durations, caches, retry counts, timeouts, and transport encodings.
- Generalized observation-free evaluation, including initially empty obligations
  and retained observation-free claims outside complete pruning.
- Cross-occurrence evidence import/reuse and operational repair, quarantine, or
  incident response for later-proven closure-integrity violations.
- Explanation projection inventory, names, omissions, fixed density-level
  count, UI layouts and interactions, and disclosure implementation.
- Concrete posture observation/recommendation/transition algorithms,
  acknowledgement and hysteresis, host enforcement, publication, and any
  Substrate or other external adopter selection.
- Delivery decomposition, acceptance planning, migration/cutover choices,
  implementation sequence, integration, feature flags, and rollout.

A later planning task must make the necessary concrete choices within the
approved semantics and obtain the applicable task-level authority. This package
does not create that task or authorize its execution.
