## Findings

### F1 [P1] Permanent exclusions fill retryable deferred state forever

Primary: `docs/research/file-native-incremental-engine-experiment-2026-10-18/src/rolling.rs:44-46,54,73-77,98-100`. Supporting: `src/lifetime.rs:66-67,125-132,220-227`; all abbreviated source names in this report are within the same engine directory above.

`eligible()` checks reservation and retirement before `mapped()`. For each retired accepted publication, both candidate/allocation bits are requeued as `reason:"retired"`, including when those records are already mapped. Retirement is permanent: `live()` checks the durable retired marker, and the lifecycle has no unretire operation. Replaying deferred work therefore requeues these bits forever. Oversize immutable role bytes have the same terminal property: their size cannot later shrink without changing immutable evidence, yet they are also requeued forever.

Concrete valid sequence: create more than 129 accepted publications, retire at least 129 old selectors while retaining a current head, then bootstrap with the default `include_attempts:true`. Even with zero readers/reservations and valid permanent outcome bytes, each retired publication consumes one deferred slot. At 128 entries the next suffix step returns `quota:deferred-eligibility-bound`; the 32 KiB serialized state bound may refuse earlier. No selected discovery progress is committed on error. Smaller chunks merely persist a finite prefix, then permanently saturate. Releasing every temporary blocker cannot restore discovery. Retirement after an earlier packing does not avoid this, because the retirement check precedes the mapped check. Setting force bypasses benefit only, not this defect; changing include_attempts on an existing cursor explicitly refuses.

This violates the required distinction between discovery progress, physical coverage and deferred eligibility, and eventual admitted drainage. It is a liveness/bookkeeping defect; no accepted-result deletion is alleged. Oversize/permanently ineligible records must remain truthfully accounted without requiring one permanent retry entry per accepted publication.

Fix: recognize already covered roles before deferral; distinguish retryable reservation/protection holes from permanent exclusions. Clear a retry when its role is covered or becomes terminally ineligible under the existing lifetime rules, while preserving accepted publication/result/hint evidence and packet ownership. Account terminal exclusions compactly under a declared immutable policy rather than dropping unknown holes or silently changing the cohort. The solution must retain the 128-entry/32 KiB discovery and 128 KiB Work limits.

Required proof: authentic >128 retired accepted selectors with live outcomes, both loose-first bootstrap and mapped-before-retirement; partial discovery with small chunks across reopen and crashes; terminal oversize cases labelled synthetic if required by fixture geometry; exact outcomes for every accepted request; snapshot refusal and saved-packet access preserved; eventual cursor completion and queue drainage after temporary reservation release. Include selected-before-transfer late eligibility and an already-deferred record retiring before retry. Do not claim the small reservation-release schedule proves terminal-exclusion progress.

### F2 [P2] Automatic benefit refusal leaves a nonresumable build owner

Primary: `src/packing.rs:575-592`; blocking follow-ups: `548-550,617-619`; explicit manual escape: `530-533` and `physical` aborted-owner adoption.

The first automatic request on a small valid disposable store writes `PACKING.json` as `status:"building"`, reserves a birth, writes/verifies pack/index output, then evaluates `automatic-compaction-benefit`. With fewer than 128 removable loose records this necessarily returns a refusal, leaving `target:None` and owned output. A subsequent build/repack refuses `unfinished-maintenance-resume-or-abort`, including after sufficient new accepted publications arrive. Resume refuses `incomplete-build-owned`. Thus a normal automatic policy decision wedges maintenance until the caller knows to explicitly abort and reclaim/adopt it. The new benefit guard causes this through a routine supported path, not an injected I/O failure.

Fix: evaluate a conservative benefit forecast before journalling/construction, or durably transition a policy-rejected candidate into the existing resumable aborted-owned lifecycle before returning. Preserve exact selected authority and ownership if death occurs during that transition. Ordinary automatic retries should not require caller repair merely because a previous cohort was too small.

Required proof: an unforced below-threshold request, then enough real accepted history for admission, followed by another unforced build without manual abort; selected bytes and all outcomes unchanged on refusal; rejected output remains owned and is eventually reclaimed with truthful credit; crash around rejection ownership transition; quota refusal distinguished from policy refusal. Keep forced-small fixtures explicitly labelled and add positive automatic admission evidence. Current smoke/schedules always use force and cannot establish this behavior.
